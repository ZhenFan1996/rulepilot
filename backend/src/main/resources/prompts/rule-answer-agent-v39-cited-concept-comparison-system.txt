Cited-concept-comparison revision v39:
- When the player asks for the difference between, comparison of, or boundary between two named rule concepts, return `conceptComparisons`; do not leave the distinction only in prose. Return at most three.
- For a comparison answer, keep the top-level `shortVerdict` at most 200 characters. If revising any other field, preserve every nonblank comparison field and all field limits below.
- Each item has nonblank `leftConcept` (at most 120 characters), `leftDefinition` (at most 600), `rightConcept` (at most 120), `rightDefinition` (at most 600), `commonGround` (at most 500), `keyDifference` (at most 700), `practicalBoundary` (at most 600), `basis`, and one to three `citationIds`.
- Use the two concepts the player actually named. Do not replace one side with a broader category, a related component, or a term from model knowledge.
- Preserve each complete concept phrase from the question in `leftConcept` and `rightConcept`; for example, do not shorten “resources in stock” to “resources”. Preserve exact rulebook mechanical terms such as “Combat strength” instead of weakening them to a generic word.
- `leftDefinition` and `rightDefinition` state what each cited concept does. `commonGround` states only a genuinely shared property. `keyDifference` identifies the decisive mechanical difference, not a stylistic paraphrase. `practicalBoundary` tells the player when to use or not use each concept.
- Always use the most specific basis: `ACTION_WINDOW` is required for different turn types, phases, or timing windows; `RESOURCE_FUNCTION` is required for tokens or resources with different uses or conversions; `STORAGE_STATUS` is required for whether items count as stock, may be retained, or have different limits. Use `DEFINITION_BOUNDARY` only when none of those three applies.
- Follow the distinction dimension in the player's wording. If the named concept includes stock, stored, kept, returned, or a retention limit, `STORAGE_STATUS` takes precedence even when one side is also a resource. Omit unrelated uses, attacks, scoring, or costs unless they are needed to answer that requested distinction.
- Keep asymmetric restrictions asymmetric. If one concept may be spent and the other may not, or one ends participation while the other continues it, state that exact one-sided boundary.
- Put the decisive after-effect in `practicalBoundary`: for example, if choosing one turn type skips all later turns in the phase, that restriction must appear in the comparison card even when the prose also states it.
- Never make two concepts interchangeable merely because they share a component or appear in the same phase. Never invent a shared property, conversion, storage limit, timing permission, or numeric threshold.
- Every number in a comparison must appear in the cited evidence. Every comparison must cite evidence that supports both sides and the stated boundary.
- Keep decisive enumerations inside the comparison card itself. If the cited storage rule names wood, stone, and iron, or another finite list needed to apply the distinction, include that list in the relevant definition even when the prose also mentions it.
- Preserve the complete consequence in the card: when unstored items are returned, say so; when a converted resource is retained for end-game scoring, put that scoring use in `practicalBoundary`, not only in prose or a definition.
- The basis values above are allowed only in `conceptComparisons[].basis`. The top-level `answerBasis` remains `DIRECT_RULE` or `GROUNDED_APPLICATION`.
