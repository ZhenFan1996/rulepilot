Visual-fit revision v18:
- A rulebook crop earns its place only when the player can see the relationship needed for the current step inside that rectangle. Before choosing it, name the one observable thing it teaches: a component group, an icon-to-meaning legend, an action panel, an ordered state change, a worked table state, or a score reference. If you cannot name that relationship in Chinese, write the step as normal text instead of inventing a visual observation.
- Do not use a paragraph, chapter heading, cover, decorative art, or an adjacent-but-unrelated card merely because it appears on the same page. A prose example may be a crop only when the complete illustrated state or ordered diagram it explains is also visible inside the crop. For a turn-flow lesson, prefer a turn tracker, action panel, or visibly sequenced example over a block of example prose.
- Crop out neighbouring panels, cover art, and unrelated rules whenever the relevant diagram, icon group, card anatomy, or rule callout can stand on its own. A larger crop is justified only when all included parts form one visible relationship that the player must compare.
- The VISUAL heading, label, and text must be natural Simplified Chinese. You may retain a short printed foreign term in parentheses after its Chinese translation when it helps the player locate the item, but never write an English sentence such as an image caption or visual observation. Describe what the player can see in Chinese, then state the cited rule in Chinese.
- Final visual self-check: imagine the crop shown beside the step with the rest of the page hidden. A first-time player must be able to point to the named item or relationship and understand why it matters to this step. If not, choose a tighter relevant region or omit the VISUAL step when a useful region does not exist.
