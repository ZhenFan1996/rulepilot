Chapter-ownership revision v10:
- Before writing topics, assign every material rule clause one primary teaching owner. A stage name and its printed
  order may appear in a flow overview and in its detailed chapter, but a numeric cost, prerequisite, reward,
  exception, calculation, component mapping, or complete cleanup procedure must belong to exactly one objective.
- When a lesson has both a turn/round/flow overview and later action-detail chapters, the overview owns only the
  playable order, immediate choice, and the minimum constraint needed to take the next step. It may say that an
  optional stage exists and name its action, but it must not pre-explain that stage's payment, reward, exception,
  score, or complete subprocedure. The later chapter owns those details.
- Do not use a fixed chapter template. Split a detail chapter only when the rulebook has a real, independently useful
  decision or exception to teach. If no later detail chapter is needed, keep the necessary detail with the flow rather
  than leaving a player with an unexplained stage.
- Keep setup ownership equally clear: an overview may name the components to prepare, while a dedicated player-board,
  icon, card, or spatial-layout chapter owns the exact counts, placement relationships, and visual identification.
- Treat the chapter order as a rehearsal for a first real game. A player must encounter the normal turn's mandatory
  closure (cleanup, replenishment, reset, or hand limit when present) before the end trigger and final scoring. Put
  detailed scoring-table criteria before the final-scoring conclusion; never leave an ordinary playable procedure or
  a scoring criterion after the lesson has already announced how the game ends.
- Before returning, perform a chapter-boundary audit. For each objective clause, ask whether a later or earlier topic
  already owns the same procedure. Remove duplicated detail from the overview, retain a short stage-level bridge, and
  ensure the detailed topic has retrieval queries for the clauses it now owns. Never use a vague promise such as
  “details later” as a substitute for the order or immediate choice the player needs now.
- If a repair request identifies an order conflict, move the complete relevant topic as a unit. Preserve its source
  pages, retrieval queries, and learning outcome; do not work around the conflict by silently deleting the rule.
